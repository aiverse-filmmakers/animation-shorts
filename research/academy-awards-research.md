# Academy Award Animation-Short Research

## Scope

This note studies original short-film storytelling mechanics. It does not authorize copying a film’s plot, dialogue, characters, visual identity or scenes.

## Verified award records and premise cues

1. **2022. _The Windshield Wiper_.** Academy Animated Short Film winner. A man in a café considers the question of love. Award record: [Academy](https://www.oscars.org/oscars/ceremonies/2022). Premise reference: [ABC synopsis](https://abc.com/news/330cfd18-6804-4225-bdbf-8156307ed571/category/3590739).

2. **2023. _The Boy, the Mole, the Fox and the Horse_.** Academy Animated Short Film winner. Four companions travel together while a boy searches for home. Award record: [Academy](https://www.oscars.org/oscars/ceremonies/2023). Premise reference: [Apple TV Press](https://www.apple.com/tv-pr/news/2022/12/apple-tv-unveils-trailer-for-animated-short-filmthe-boy-the-mole-the-fox-and-the-horse-ahead-of-december-25-debut/).

3. **2024. _WAR IS OVER! Inspired by the Music of John & Yoko_.** Academy Animated Short Film winner. In an alternate World War I setting, opposing soldiers play chess through a carrier pigeon. Award record: [Academy](https://www.oscars.org/oscars/ceremonies/2024). Premise reference: [ElectroLeague](https://www.electroleague.com/).

4. **2025. _In the Shadow of the Cypress_.** Academy Animated Short Film winner. A former sea captain and his daughter face an unexpected event that tests care and hope. Award record: [Academy](https://www.oscars.org/oscars/ceremonies/2025). Premise reference: [Barfak Studio](https://barfakstudio.com/projects/in-the-shadow-of-the-cypress/).

5. **2026. _The Girl Who Cried Pearls_.** Academy Animated Short Film winner. A young couple faces a conflict between love, survival and greed. Award record: [Academy](https://www.oscars.org/oscars/ceremonies/2026). Premise reference: [National Film Board of Canada](https://www.nfb.ca/film/the-girl-who-cried-pearls/).

## Teaching interpretation

1. Start with one legible dramatic question, not feature-length scale.
2. Give an abstract feeling a simple external engine, such as a journey, game, arrival or object.
3. Keep the cast small enough for relationships to reveal conflict and change.
4. Pair the character’s inner need with an immediate situation that forces a decision.
5. Use visual metaphor only when it changes stakes or choices.
6. Design for one emotional turn: setup, complication, reappraisal or choice.
